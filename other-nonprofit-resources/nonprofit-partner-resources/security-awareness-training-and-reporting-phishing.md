# Security Awareness Training and Reporting Phishing

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Partners only.** Good Heart Tech provides this program at no cost to eligible partner nonprofits. Not a partner yet? See [Free IT Support from Good Heart Tech](../../free-it-support-from-good-heart-tech.md).
{% endhint %}

## What this is

**Phin Security** runs short security awareness training and occasional **practice phishing emails** for your organization. **Good Heart Tech** configures Phin with your **Microsoft 365** or **Google Workspace** mail.

* **Training reminders** arrive in your inbox. Open the email and follow the link. No separate password to remember.
* **Practice phishing** messages are **safe simulations**. They help you learn what to look for. They are not real attacks.
* If you click a practice message by mistake, you may see a short **Learning Moment** with tips. No shame, just learning.

## Real email vs practice

| | Practice (Phin) | Suspicious or real threat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Purpose** | Teach | Harm you or your organization |
| **What to do** | Learn from it; no need to panic | Pause, report, ask IT if unsure |

When something feels wrong, **slow down** before you click links, open attachments, or reply with passwords or gift card codes.

## Report in Microsoft Outlook (Microsoft 365)

Good Heart Tech installs Phin’s **Report Phishing** tools for partner organizations on Microsoft 365.

1. Open the message in Outlook (desktop or web).
2. Use the **Report Phishing** button (ribbon or message toolbar, depending on your Outlook version).
3. Follow the prompts.

Your **welcome email** from the training program includes Phin’s article and short video on the Outlook Report Phishing button. Save that email or ask your admin for a copy if you need the links again.

Outlook also has Microsoft’s own **Report** options on the Home tab or when you right-click a message. See [Managing Mail in Outlook (Microsoft 365)](../../microsoft/microsoft-365-user-guides/managing-mail-in-outlook-microsoft-365.md).

**When in doubt, report it.** That protects your organization and helps us spot real threats early.

## Report in Gmail (Google Workspace)

Google includes **Report phishing** in Gmail. You do not need a separate Phin button in Gmail.

1. Open the message in Gmail.
2. Click the **three dots** (**More**) at the top right of the message.
3. Click **Report phishing**.
4. Confirm if Gmail asks.

Gmail may warn you if the sender looks risky. Reporting helps Google and your administrators protect everyone on your domain.
